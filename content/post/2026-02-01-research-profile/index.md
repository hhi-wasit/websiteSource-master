My research focuses on how communities can participate effectively in the surveillance of infectious diseases and how public health researchers can measure the social, behavioural, and organisational factors that shape that participation.

During my PhD at Universiti Sains Malaysia, I designed and conducted a cross-sectional epidemiological study on community-based infectious disease surveillance in Malaysia. The work included the development, adaptation, and validation of questionnaire instruments for community leaders and representatives.

### Methods

The research programme applies exploratory factor analysis, confirmatory factor analysis, Item Response Theory, structural equation modelling, path analysis, and epidemiological study design. These methods support the development of valid instruments and the analysis of relationships between knowledge, attitudes, perceptions, self-efficacy, perceived barriers, perceived benefits, intention, and community participation.

### Research interests

My current research interests include infectious disease epidemiology, infectious disease modelling, public health surveillance, community engagement, community participation, instrument development, questionnaire adaptation and validation, and quantitative evaluation of public health programmes.

My software experience includes **R, Mplus, JAMOVI, JASP, and SPSS**.
