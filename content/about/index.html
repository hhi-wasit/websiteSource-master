The Long Version

I am Ahmed Azeez, a lecturer and public health researcher based in Iraq. My academic work focuses on community-based surveillance of infectious diseases and the social, behavioural, and measurement factors that influence community participation in public health programmes.

I completed a PhD in Community Medicine and Public Health at Universiti Sains Malaysia from 2021 to 2025, specialising in the epidemiology of infectious diseases. During my doctoral research, I designed and conducted a cross-sectional epidemiological study on community-based infectious disease surveillance in Malaysia. I also developed, adapted, and validated questionnaire instruments for community leaders and representatives using exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis, Item Response Theory, structural equation modelling, and path analysis.

Before the PhD, I completed a Master’s Degree in Community Health at Middle Technical University in Baghdad from 2015 to 2018, and a Bachelor’s Degree in Community Health at the same university from 2006 to 2010.

In my current role as a Lecturer at the Higher Health Institute of Wasit, I teach undergraduate students in emergency health, public health, and epidemiology. I deliver lectures on infectious disease surveillance and research methods, supervise student research, provide academic mentoring, and contribute to curriculum development and other academic activities.

My earlier professional experience includes work as a Community Health Specialist in the Maternal and Child Health Division of the Public Health Department of Wasit from 2018 to 2021. In that role, I trained health workers and doctors in Antenatal Care, Integrated Management of Neonatal and Child Health, and Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care. From 2010 to 2015, I worked as a Public Health Officer in the Public Health Department of Wasit across communicable diseases, health visitor, medical, pharmacy, antenatal, and postnatal care units.

Research interests

My research interests include infectious disease epidemiology, infectious disease modelling, community-based disease surveillance, community engagement and participation, public health programme evaluation, epidemiological study design, questionnaire adaptation and validation, and the application of advanced quantitative and psychometric methods to public health research.

I use R, Mplus, JAMOVI, JASP, and SPSS for statistical analysis and research. I am also a WHO-certified e-instructor and hold a Training of Trainers qualification for health-worker training.

Selected scholarly work

My publications during the PhD period examine community engagement and participation in community-based infectious disease surveillance, the development and validation of knowledge, attitudes, and perceptions questionnaires, and structural equation modelling of social and behavioural factors. My scholarly outputs include articles in the Journal of Public Health Research, Osong Public Health and Research Perspectives, the International Journal of One Health, and the Medical Journal of Babylon, as well as a published book chapter.

My ongoing research includes a Bayesian hierarchical and negative binomial regression analysis of the spatiotemporal dynamics of four zoonotic public health threats in Wasit Governorate, Iraq, and the cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation of an Arabic Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices questionnaire for breast cancer screening.
